Zanketsu Vision: 7 Interconnected Systems Reshaping the Arab Digital Infrastructure

Before discussing technology in Yemen, a question must be asked frankly: How can a country where less than one-fifth of the population uses the internet think about building advanced digital systems? The answer begins with understanding the true size of the gap separating Yemen from its Arab and global surroundings—a gap that is measured not by optimism, but by explicit figures showing the actual scale of the challenge.

Today, Yemen records an internet penetration rate of no more than 17.7% of the total population, meaning that over 82% of Yemenis do not use the internet at all. This figure alone puts Yemen far behind the Arabian Gulf states, which lead the region in digital adoption rates.

These pain points are not marginal details; they represent the exact context in which the idea of Zanketsu was born. Any tech company attempting to operate in Yemen today faces a fundamental question: Do you build a single simple product to solve a specific problem, or do you build a broader vision that recognizes Yemen's problems are interconnected and cannot be resolved with isolated solutions?

The core concept behind this interconnection is that economic and social sectors in Yemen do not operate in isolation. A fitness center needs an administrative system, which in turn needs a reliable payment system, which in turn requires a sovereign financial infrastructure that does not collapse at the first political crisis. When these systems are built separately by different companies that do not coordinate, the same fragmentation occurs.

Building an integrated ecosystem means that every new solution is designed with the rest of the systems in mind. When a business owner logs into the Zanketsu platform, they have a single unified account representing their identity across the entire platform, instead of fragmented accounts that need to be managed separately.

The vision for 2030 is not based on exaggerated promises that Yemen will catch up with the Gulf states or Europe in a few years. Rather, it is based on a more realistic and achievable goal: that despite all these circumstances, Yemen will possess a solid local digital infrastructure in core sectors that does not wait for political stability to function, but is built from the ground up to endure and persist despite these difficult conditions.
